# Companion idle-animation design

This is a design reference for the additional actions tracked in the [current roadmap](../ROADMAP.md), not a separate task tracker. Existing five-state reactions and shared reading, writing, and page turning remain in place. The user approved the first-pass in-app mood transition. These new idle ideas are proposed; implement and review them individually.

## Happy

### Humming to himself

1. Read for a moment, then look up slightly.
2. Soften into a contented smile and make small rhythmic humming mouth shapes.
3. Gently tilt his head in rhythm, with the tuft following slightly behind.
4. Show one or two musical notes in sequence; let them float upward and disappear.
5. Pause, smile to himself, and return his eyes to the book.

The expression and motion make the humming visible; no audio is required. Avoid rapid, repetitive head bobbing and a constant stream of notes.

### Delighted by a passage

Read intently, pause over a line, then brighten and smile. Briefly draw the book closer, with a couple of twinkling stars, before settling back into reading.

## Waiting

### Searching for his place

Scan across the page, stop, and look back slightly. Tilt his head, follow the line again, then give a small satisfied nod when he finds his place. Keep the book steady.

### Quietly thinking

Pause and look up to one side. Shift his eyes as if considering something; show the existing thinking dots and question mark in sequence. Hold, let the mark disappear, and return to the page. Build on the existing thinking action with varied pauses.

## Hungry

### Distracted by the thought of food

Try to read, then let his eyes drift away. Form a thought bubble with steaming food and hold his hopeful gaze briefly. As the bubble disappears, give a weary sigh and return to the book. Develop the existing food-thought sequence without introducing a smile.

### Stomach rumble

Pause mid-read, look toward his stomach, and react to a small rumble mark. Rest a hand against himself briefly, lower it, and resume reading more slowly. Keep the expression tired and hungry and the movement subtle.

## Sad

### Losing focus

Read slowly, let his eyes stop, and hold the pause. Glance up as if waiting for someone, then lower his gaze and retreat a little closer to the book. Let the tuft and bookmark settle afterward. This action does not need a tear.

### Comforting himself with the book

Pause, gently draw the book closer, and rest against it. After a quiet hold and slow breath, ease back into the reading position. If used, a tear should appear very rarely rather than in every loop.

## New

### Curious peek

While reading with his head down, notice something and slowly peek over the book. Look toward the viewer, hold the curious gaze, blink, and return to reading. Keep this smaller than the existing surprise-and-wave greeting.

### Getting absorbed in the book

Move his eyes visibly from left to right and back. Lean a little closer, pause at the end of a line, and relax back into the reading posture.

## Acting and sequencing

- Give every action readable anticipation, gesture, hold, recovery, and appropriate follow-through. Hungry and Sad generally move more slowly than Happy.
- Vary the intervals of quiet reading and the next action; avoid repeating symbols or humming on a short fixed loop.
- Keep the eyes, face, monocle, tuft, hands, book, and bookmark connected. Maintain one continuous arm per side and do not add a finger grip. Keep the body extending behind the book.
- Cancel the outgoing action on a mood change and use the approved transition. Avoid whole-character fades, flashing, abrupt pose jumps, and excessive body sway.
- Preserve complete animation sequences and ensure gestures remain readable at widget size. Widget validation remains deferred under roadmap item 3; do not claim it is complete.
- Start with Happy humming and obtain visual review before proceeding to the next sequence.
